\subsection*{(b) Hyperparameter Search}

\textbf{Search:} Ray Tune evaluated all 25 pairs of $h\in\{16,32,64,128,256\}$ and $d\in\{2,4,8,12,16\}$, using the same $d$ for four categorical features. Training used 2017--2020; validation used 2021. Settings were seed 42, Adam, learning rate 0.01, batch size 512, maximum 20 epochs, and early stopping with patience 4 and minimum validation RMSE improvement $10^{-4}$.

\subsection*{(e) Discussion}

\textbf{Capacity:} The selected configuration improved validation RMSE over $(64,8)$ (63,422.46 SGD) by 5.76\%. The runner-up $(256,2)$ scored 59,926.48 SGD, only 159.58 SGD higher. Larger capacity did not consistently help: at $h=128$, $d=16$ scored 65,599.97 SGD.

\textbf{Fit and generalisation:} From epochs 13 to 17, training RMSE declined from 31,207.16 to 30,627.12 SGD while validation RMSE rose from 59,766.91 to 69,319.56 SGD, indicating late overfitting and supporting early stopping. The final training/test gap suggests limited generalisation; temporal changes may contribute, so the gap alone cannot isolate overfitting. The curves do not suggest simple capacity-based underfitting.

\subsection*{(a) Removing Categorical Embeddings}

\textbf{Change:} Remove all four embedding tables and change the first layer from $54\to128$ to $6\to128$, retaining LayerNorm, ReLU and output layer. Parameters decrease from 8,649 to 1,281.

\textbf{Result:} Mean test RMSE rose 14.15\%; mean $R^2$ fell from 0.6543 to 0.5492. Four of five paired seeds worsened. Mean training RMSE rose from 38,042.36 to 54,773.49 SGD, supporting the value of categorical information; information loss and reduced parameter count are confounded.
\subsection*{(b) Replacing ReLU with Sigmoid}

\textbf{Change:} Replace only the hidden activation, retaining all inputs and 8,649 parameters.

\textbf{Result:} Mean test RMSE fell 2.58\%, but SD increased from 3,652.75 to 7,310.91 SGD; three of five paired seeds improved. Mean training RMSE was similar (38,248.06 versus 38,042.36 SGD). The bounded activation changes representations and gradients; the modest mean improvement and larger variation do not establish a reliable advantage over ReLU.

\subsection*{(c) Discussion}

\textbf{Mechanism and evidence:} The linear branch offers a shorter gradient path to shared embeddings and may learn additive patterns alongside nonlinear MLP relationships. All positive coefficients had worse validation RMSE, providing no evidence of a benefit under these settings.

\textbf{Limitations:} Gradient norms were not measured, so relief of vanishing gradients cannot be verified. Coefficient selection used one seed, and final testing with $\lambda=0$ cannot establish how positive coefficients generalise.
